## 3. Decision gates

A gate is a point where a task waits for a ruling. Following §18.70,
gates come in two kinds. **Author decisions** (§3.A) are language or
policy questions: the author decides, on the evidence listed.
**Technical gates** (§3.B) are settled by evidence and a reviewed
proposal and are not posed to the author as language-design
questions. A technical gate closes when its evidence is committed and
the named reviewer reports no blocking finding; the closure, with the
review, is recorded in docs/findings.md and reported to the author,
who may reopen it. The sign-off for a technical gate is the named
reviewer's report of no blocking finding; the author is informed of
the closure, not asked to rule it. "T-R0a's owner" and "T-R0b's
owner" name documents, not people (§18.69 Q11 makes T-R0b, a
document, the owner of `ValidR`'s meaning). In this plan an owner's
agreement to a technical change means: the task's implementer edits
the R0 document, the gate's independent reviewer checks the edit
against the document's other statements, and the change is reported
to the author with the closure. If a review finds that a technical gate in fact
turns on a language or policy point, that point moves to §3.A with
the finding. Some questions have both halves (G-erased, G-ValidR,
G-exempt); each half is listed where it belongs. No gate has a
default unless the R0 documents state one, and this plan assumes
none.

### 3.2 Checker limitations stay local to the checker

A limitation of one checker must not become a global rule against
other strategies (§18.70). Two cases are kept apart, and only the
first blocks Solved for every strategy.

(a) **An incomplete recorded problem: the support boundary.** The
recorded problem does not state every acceptance-relevant
obligation, or the specification leaves an atom's meaning open. This
covers `outside` markers (rule 1), a second spread (rule 1′), the
erased-row marker once G-erased rules it, and, before G-ValidR, a
`BQ` atom at a declared type whose constructors hold functions or at
a fresh base, whose meaning `ValidR` does not yet fix. No strategy
can be sound for an obligation the problem omits or the
specification leaves undefined, so these block Solved whichever
strategy accepts, with reason `Unsupported` (the `Outside` kinds,
`SecondSpread`, or `RequirementAtoms`). Every strategy still runs,
as rule 1 requires. The pre-G-ValidR block is keyed on the open set
G-ValidR names, not on G0, and lapses atom by atom as G-ValidR rules.

(b) **A complete requirement that G0 cannot discharge.** A `BQ` atom
that `entailedGround` (Task 4.2) leaves mentioning a rigid variable
`D` does not decide (AT19's third case; `show(item)` at a rigid `a`
with empty `Q`), including one outside the unitary fragment where
`entailedGround` answers "not ground". The problem states the
obligation; G0 has no rule for it. The limitation is local to the
strategies whose `check` is G0 (in this plan, `online`): their
`guard` returns `OutOfScope RequirementAtoms`, so the attempt is
`Declined online RequirementAtoms`. It is not a rule-1 block, not a
`CheckFailed`, and not a `SearchFault`. Another injected strategy
whose own guard and trusted check cover the atom may accept it
(Solved); a trusted refuter or registered rule may refute it within
its own proved scope (Task 5.3's scope excludes `BQ`, §18.69 Q10,
which is that refuter's limit, not a rule on others). When no
strategy accepts and every strategy declined, rule 3's existing
"every strategy declined" gives `Unsupported [RequirementAtoms]`,
which is AT19's third case. That is the path from G0's limit to
`Unsupported` that §8 item 8 found missing, now without a global
rule.

`BQ` atoms that σ grounds are still judged inside G0's `check` under
σ (AT19's second case); a failure there refuses that candidate and is
never Contradiction. Two kinds of atom are left out of that judgement
because their meaning is open, which is §3.2(a), not G0's limit
(revised after the re-review of 38c045e). First, G0's `check` does
not judge the atoms in `openRequirements(P)` (Task 4.2), such as
`main`'s Sig10 atom at a declared type holding functions: they
already block Solved through rule 1, and G0 neither accepts nor
refuses on them. Second, an atom that becomes such a type only under
σ (a flexible variable σ binds to a declared type holding functions
or to a fresh base) is not in `openRequirements(P)`, which is
computed on the recorded problem; G0 refuses that candidate with
`RequirementOpen`. That refusal is not a `SearchFault`, and rule 3
reads it as `Unsupported RequirementAtoms` when no strategy accepts
(Task 4.1). RT8 lists both as expected classes labelled (a). Only
when G0's `check` meets an atom its guard admitted and that is in
neither kind, and it cannot judge it, does it refuse with
`RequirementUnjudged`. Guard and check of one strategy then disagree,
which is a defect in that strategy, so RT8 counts it as a defect, not
as an expected class.

Tests (Tasks 4.1, 4.2): `a G0 limit is local to G0`, where `pr a`
at a rigid `a` gives `Unsupported [RequirementAtoms]` with only
`online` injected and Solved when a stub strategy whose guard admits
it and whose test-minted check accepts is added; `a refuter may judge
a G0-declined atom`, where a test-only registered rule refuting the
atom gives Contradiction; `an incomplete problem blocks every
strategy`, where a marker or a pre-G-ValidR atom with an accepting
stub gives `Unsupported`. Regression row `r0-g0-limit-global` (Task
4.2) turns G0's decline back into a rule-1 block, and the probe
expects the stub's Solved.

## 8. Problems in the R0 documents found while planning

1. **R0 as decided narrows what compiles today, through several

     whole-declaration Solved): `DataApplication` (T-R0b §11 item 4:
     any `Option(a)` use), `DataOverRecords` (Sig8 signatures),

     the dropped obligations are accounted for is open (T-R0b §11
     item 6); no task implemented it before this revision (G-erased,
     Task 0.7);
